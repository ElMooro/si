What these measurements can tell you

Rate dispersion uses 30 successive numeric-observation yield changes in basis points. Currency and equity dispersion use 20 log changes in percent. Annualization assumes 252 comparable steps. Reported implied-index levels remain separate; a Treasury statistic never substitutes for MOVE, and an average of FX volatilities is not portfolio volatility.

Each descriptive z-score and midrank percentile compares an estimate with 504 preceding estimates, excluding itself. Overlapping windows are dependent. A flat baseline has no defined z-score; missing or invalid windows prevent a clean baseline. These statistics do not establish a migration forecast or a cheap hedge.

Source acquisition and observation lag are separate. Acquisitions expire after 26 hours; observation ceilings are 12 days for weekly-published H.10 FX data and seven days for other sources. These are explicit review limits, not a verified release calendar. Same-day quotes need matching provider-reported session times and a capture at least 30 minutes after the reported close. Official-feed parity remains unverified.

Full returned history is the acquired current vintage. It is not a point-in-time backtest. The browser checks immutable output and source hashes; a separate Python replay reconstructs all historical calculations and checks their arithmetic independently.

Portfolio consequence: unavailable. WAIT means abstain. Predictive validation, portfolio holdings, covariance, costs and risk constraints are required before these measurements can set target weights or trade permissions.
